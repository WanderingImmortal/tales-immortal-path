# Tales of the Immortal Path mobile UI and UX audit

Audit of commit [`307bf21`](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/commit/307bf21c8c7edf96582b866865e840b0d88b8da1) · 27 November 2026

## Executive summary

The default mobile layout presents the desktop sidebar before the game world. At 390 × 844, the Redwell scene starts about 1,980 CSS pixels down the document and the Event Log starts about 3,205 pixels down. The opt-in phone layout restores a usable scene-first order and keeps the log nearby. It also introduces small calendar controls, a dock that covers content at short heights, and a second way to dismiss overlays. Neither layout currently provides a dependable path through every core interaction.

First prevent Back from hiding live seclusion controls, restore access to Dantian at 320 × 568, make the map tabs fully tappable, and keep the phone dock clear of Cultivate in landscape. Next, establish common overlay behavior and make calendar, activity and event feedback easy to read while playing. Use the existing phone shell as the starting point. Keep the ink-dark surfaces, muted gold, serif typography, chamber art and map illustrations. The game needs a clearer hierarchy and stronger interaction rules within that visual style.

## Scope and method

I served the shipped static browser game locally and used Playwright with Chromium 156, isolated saves, touch emulation, real control activation and DOM geometry and hit-testing. I checked fresh creation and the main loop on the default layout at 320 × 568, 375 × 667 and 390 × 844, and on the phone layout at 320 × 568, 390 × 844, 430 × 932 and 844 × 390 landscape. The desktop comparison was 1440 × 1000, with additional breakpoint probes at 840 px height. The four existing desktop Playwright smoke tests passed. The external webfont was blocked for repeatability, so screenshots use the system font fallbacks specified in CSS. Measurements reflect the tested viewport, content state and fonts. Mobile operating system chrome, safe-area insets, keyboards and assistive technology require physical device confirmation.

The ordinary-save pass included creation and the First Steps lesson, both main layouts, character settings and stats, the Event Log, the Three Refinements hub and all three chambers, an actual Gather Qi action, action help, focused cultivation start and cancel, exploration, live and paused calendar, world and region maps, Redwell local map, inventory, techniques, the empty pills state, forge and its Repair tab, an alchemy brew attempt, diary, a market purchase, the seclusion duration picker, playback Pause, speed and Back, work, lodging, Well-Ring and City Lord entry screens, NPC discovery and its detail menu, an actual local walk to Brine-Well Hamlet, and the combat board, technique drawer and an attack input. The early sect sheet showed gated entries. A separate QA save used the game's built-in realm and resource grants and action-gate toggle for Intent, Meridians, Factions, the Forbidden Grounds entry, Core Formation Dantian, local destination, sect founding and the resulting sect grounds view. The QA pass sampled those layouts with the corresponding unlock conditions bypassed.

Coverage stops before full NPC conversations, paid work and lodge upgrades, full alchemy and forging progression, combat resolution and rewards, seclusion completion and its highlight reel, advanced Body and Soul layers, Dao, breakthrough and tribulation choices, transcendence, reincarnation, and deeper faction, sect and forbidden-zone branches. Separate combat, alchemy and sect prototypes were outside production-game scope. The audit documents the tested revision and proposes follow-up work. The [screenshot index](mobile-ui-ux-audit/README.md) contains all 88 captures.

## Prioritised findings

**P0** blocks a core interaction in a tested viewport. **P1** substantially degrades a common flow or accessibility. **P2** is a narrower improvement. Each finding identifies a defect, a UX design problem, or an aesthetic preference.

### P0 · Three Refinements can lose its title and first chamber · Defect

At 390 × 844, the hub shell starts at y = −65 and the title is above the viewport in both modes. At 320 × 568 in phone mode, the shell starts at y = −514 and the Dantian card is entirely offscreen. Touch activation fails even after a scroll-into-view attempt. The Soul Palace card wraps its description into a narrow column, making the overall failure worse. The fixed overlay bottom-aligns the hub at phone widths. Its custom shell has no maximum height or internal scroll, and the fourth grid child, the card note, auto-places into the icon column. Give the hub a bounded surface with a scrolling body. Place the note explicitly with the title and description and keep the icon column intrinsically narrow. This is a release blocker for a phone-first layout. ### P0 · Phone dock covers the main action in landscape · Defect

At 844 × 390, the fixed log and dock start at y = 244. Cultivate sits at y = 264–304 underneath them and the scene gets a 42 px scroll region at first paint. ### P0 · Back hides live seclusion controls while years continue passing · Defect

On the 390 × 844 phone layout, I started a 10-year seclusion, paused, selected 3× speed, resumed and tapped the floating Back. The overlay disappeared, including Pause and Emerge. The playback still reported active and age advanced from 192 to 240 months during the check. The shared Escape close table routes playback to `hideTimePlaybackOverlay()`, which only changes visibility. Emerge follows a separate stop-and-settle path. Treat active seclusion as a committed surface and keep its explicit controls available. Any future minimize behavior needs an equally visible resume or Emerge control and a reviewed policy on continued time. ### P1 · Map controls are clipped and the local map is hard to tap · Defect and UX design problem

At 390 × 844, the World layer gave the tab strip a 9 px box for 44 px buttons. The center of the World tab hit the content below it. Tab content could sometimes be activated through the exposed sliver or after scrolling. The tall content and flex shrink combine with horizontal auto overflow, which also makes the strip a clipping box in the vertical axis. Give tabs a non-shrinking header row and a single scrolling content region. Local map pins use absolute positions and 9–10 px labels in dense mode. A 2-month walk to Brine-Well Hamlet worked after selecting its pin. Add a compact destinations list with the same selections, gates, costs and travel command as the illustrated map. Show the selected destination and actionable cost together. Preserve the World → Region → Local hierarchy and the richer desktop view. ### P1 · Dismissal, focus and scroll ownership disagree across surfaces · Defect and UX design problem

Opening the World sheet leaves focus on its launcher. Tab can reach other dock buttons and the floating Back before entering the dialog, and Close returns focus to the document body. Diary and the Stats Guide open above their sheets, while Map and Inventory replace theirs. This difference follows a `.details-toggle` CSS-class check in a click listener. A chamber Return goes straight to the game because the hub closes when the chamber opens. Browser Back left the game URL with the map open. In combat, Escape correctly kept the fight open, but it also left the optional Techniques child open. The phone has an additional floating Back with its own overlay scan and fallback `.active` removal, which risks bypassing close callbacks. Most popup roots lack dialog semantics and focus handling. The phone-scoped popup styles cannot match the body-level overlays, and custom chamber shells also use different classes from `.popup-box`. The phone padding selector `.layout-phone-playtest #app` has the same scope problem because the class is on `#app` itself. Keep the explicit mandatory-flow rules. Make Back and Escape resolve the top surface, return to the correct parent, and restore focus and scroll. Treat browser history as a product choice with save-ownership testing. ### P1 · Calendar and active work are hard to read, control and verify · Defect and UX design problem

On the phone shell at 390 × 844, Play and the speed buttons are 16 px high with 9 px type. Cancel is 22 px high. Starting focused cultivation pushes the scene down about 65 px and repeats activity context in the calendar label and expanded activity row. Opening Inventory during the session stopped clock advancement while the activity still reported “Running.” The activity derives state from requested play and the clock label only updates when its renderer runs, so a freeze transition can be visually stale. Foraging can be toggled twice only by returning to World, and fight seeking uses the small activity line. Make Pause a persistent, thumb-reachable control. Display activity, effective state (paused, running or held), progress and Stop or Cancel together where those actions already exist. Keep speed and its time units available through a readable segmented control or a labeled details view. Surface a small result after Gather Qi or a transaction inside the current interface, and keep the Event Log available for history. The log renderer replaces every row and sets `scrollTop` to zero, so a new event or full render can interrupt reading older entries. Preserve calendar timing, interruptions and existing action availability. ### P1 · Small touch targets and hover-only cues hide critical information · Accessibility defects

Provide text for lock reasons, named controls and semantic selection. Give the anatomical view a synchronized region list, and use a separate feedback region for noteworthy outcomes so assistive technology does not repeatedly read the entire log. ### P2 · System emoji dilute visual consistency · Aesthetic preference with discoverability impact

Multicolor emoji are used in navigation, headings, stats, map art, combat, item data, CSS pseudo-elements and gameplay log strings. They mix modern imagery such as a backpack and hard hat with cultivated motifs and render differently across platforms. Introduce one small semantic icon registry at the presentation layer. Pilot a folded map, scroll, medicine gourd, spirit stone, forge hammer, crossed blades and cultivation seal on navigation and common actions. Use simple locally bundled monochrome vector symbols sized to the type scale. Pair them with visible labels in the dock and hide decorative symbols from assistive technology. Reuse the same glyph for the same action across screens. Review color and state cues for meaning in text. Treat decorative or narrative emoji as a later art decision, and keep existing item data and saved log text readable throughout migration. ## Reusable mobile UI architecture

### Use one game shell with responsive placements

Keep one set of action handlers and UI state. Start from the existing phone shell's identity, action categories and reusable calendar and log nodes. Give the shell a measured status header, a scene or active destination with one scroll owner, and a footer with an event preview and labeled navigation. Use `minmax(0, 1fr)`, `min-inline-size: 0`, intrinsic sizing and logical safe-area padding. Define compact behavior for short heights independently of widths. Apply the phone layout via a single resolver for viewport and saved preference, and make stylesheet scope reach the overlay host explicitly. ### Manage overlays through one lifecycle

Adopt shared variants for compact choice dialogs, scrollable sheets and full-screen workspaces. Give each a real header, one scrollable body and optional action footer. ## Developer decisions before implementation

1. **Default mobile layout and supported sizes.** Decide when narrow screens should adopt the phone shell, whether the current opt-in preference stays during rollout, and whether 320 px portrait and short landscape are first-class layouts. Recommended rollout is opt-in until the core journeys and desktop comparisons pass, followed by a separate default change.
2. **Navigation and shortcut policy.** Choose the labeled dock destinations and the priority between Map, Inventory, Explore and the calendar. Decide whether Cultivate always opens Three Refinements or also offers a last-used chamber shortcut. Decide where the Return action lands after a chamber or a destination sheet.
3. **Dismissal and history.** Agree whether operating system or browser Back dismisses an optional surface or follows page history, whether Back steps through map layers, and which sheets allow outside dismissal. Decide whether seclusion may ever minimize with time running. Confirm how mandatory choices remain protected plus how exit and save ownership behave when leaving the page.
4. **Calendar and logs.** Choose what is always visible, whether speed gets a details view, what the event preview shows, and when activity controls remain reachable inside chambers. Displaying held state is a correction. Any change to when a menu pauses the simulation is a gameplay decision.
5. **Density and art.** Choose the status shown by default on the scene, which creation choices start collapsed, and how much illustration space combat and chambers reserve on short screens. Confirm whether Local defaults to the map or destinations list. Approve the first icon families, line weight and color rules. Permit narrative emoji case by case if they are part of the voice.
