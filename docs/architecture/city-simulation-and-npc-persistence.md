# City simulation and NPC persistence

**Status** — Proposal for review. Baseline is [merged main at `6fcbb73`](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/commit/6fcbb731af096a642f19124d36909994cf1f4c67), including the [post-stabilization audit](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md). This proposal authorizes no implementation or balance changes.

## Recommendation

The player should feel that every inhabitant has a life of their own. A player can approach ordinary people they encounter in a city, and any of those people can become recognizable through play. Most inhabitants contribute to city life through aggregate conditions and events. When a person comes into view, their circumstances should fit the city, and more detail should emerge naturally as the player or the world gives them a reason to matter.

Use settlements as the owners of slow local developments. Keep people, organisations, offices, and physical inventories under their existing owners. City conditions shape the people and opportunities encountered there. Individuals and organisations can in turn change those conditions. Across reincarnations, later lives should eventually encounter the accumulated consequences of earlier lives.

The urgent first prerequisite was protecting the active successor from budget-driven retirement. That guard has shipped. The next proposed work is a small encounter-to-identity investigation, followed by one offscreen Redwell development on the existing calendar. Cross-life continuity is a design constraint from the start, with a separate implementation and recovery review. The first pilots can use existing NPC, office, and incident systems. A general-purpose agent framework and a full economy can wait.

### What the cap actually does

`enforcePersistentCap()` compares a global threshold of 100 to the living persistent rows with a meeting timestamp and no current player-sect affiliation. On the audited baseline, low impression and low talk count could lead directly to `alive = false`. The cap leaves the row in the roster and bypasses the ordinary death path. The separate per-zone ambient cap is 10, and refreshing a crowd can also mark old ambient rows dead directly. These limits do not bound the total saved roster or measure the cost of detailed simulation. [data.js](../../data.js#L3351) · [ambient caps on main](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/blob/main/ambient-npcs.js#L175) · [npc-presence.js](../../npc-presence.js#L58)

On the audited baseline, a successor with one conversation and low impression could be selected at a full cap. Living UID lookup then failed while both the game-over and succession flags were false. This was an urgent identity correctness defect independent of the city proposal. Merged PR #254 protects the controlled successor, travellers, and actors with activity claims. Its regression also checks that the active successor retains their cultivation object after save/load. Current main still marks other eligible persistent actors dead, so the general lifecycle problem remains. Low talk count can coexist with a durable relationship, office or obligation. [Historical audit, A-01](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md#a-01--high--population-retirement-can-kill-the-player-controlled-successor) · [PR #254](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/pull/254) · [Current regression](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/blob/main/scripts/test-unified-disciple-identity.js#L1408)

**Other verified boundaries.** Civic holder rotation happens in a getter-driven ensure, so reading an office after a jump can trigger the change instead of a dated civic event. Scene presence can also seed zone NPCs through `getWorldNpcsInZone()`. The old audit reproduced an NPC ceiling changing with player cultivation focus. These are concrete dependencies. The cost of a realistic century of city simulation and the frequency of serious civic upheaval are unknown. ## Proposed model

### Settlement state and authority

Use the existing settlement location ID as the key. A settlement can include an authored hinterland with links to its contributing sites. A zone may contain multiple cities, outposts, wild sites, sects, and mobile groups. Treating every location or an entire zone as one city would attribute their actors and stock incorrectly. A wilderness source or a roaming cultivator may have no city owner.

Begin with a small settlement state under the existing world root. Store its last evaluated calendar period, one durable development, and only the aggregate or policy inputs that this development needs. Keep starting conditions, capability limits, and allowed institutions in the settlement profile. Read market conditions, offices, and known regional shocks from their owning systems. Add coarse population or cultivation bands when a concrete development requires them. Prosperity, order, and effectiveness can begin as derived observations. They need independent stored meters only if play requires distinct causes and responses.

| Owner | Proposed authority |
|---|---|
| Calendar | Existing `G.ageMonths` and scheduled events. A settlement’s evaluation date is a checkpoint on the shared calendar. |
| Settlement | Local conditions, aggregate background population where modeled, durable policy or pressure records, and a bounded public consequence history. |
| Person | One NPC UID for mutable identity, progression, runtime location, and personal history. Player control uses the same successor identity. |
| Office or organisation | Its institutional identity, policies, and membership or occupant references. Existing named civic holders can remain embedded until a feature requires an NPC UID. Once promoted, the seat retains the UID and adapters derive copies for old readers. |
| Stocks and claims | Existing resource sources, inventories and procurement ledgers own finite amounts. Existing travel, Hall, mission and situation records own their commitments. City state refers to these records. |

City summaries should be read-only. Initialization and evaluation should have explicit simulation entry points, so a city does not advance faster because the player visits its panel more often.

### From encountering a person to changing the world

These are five capabilities that can overlap. They can share an actor record and need no fixed class hierarchy or mandatory progression. A lone encounter may start a lasting relationship. An offscreen officeholder may already have influence when the player first meets them. Temporary detail changes should be seamless in play, with the same recognizable person and history before and after.

| Capability | Proposed behaviour |
|---|---|
| Instantiate for an encounter | Let the player approach people presented in streets, markets or other scenes. Generate enough detail for action and recognition from local circumstances, with an encounter reference that stays stable throughout the scene. |
| Retain identity and established facts | Keep a compact record and stable UID when familiarity accumulates or a consequential encounter makes the person worth tracking. Repeated conversations can suffice without a quest or exceptional status. Preserve the encounter UID when promoting a row that already has one. |
| Run lightweight autonomous activity | Give selected people a dated objective, a routine, or a next meaningful review. A known person with no active pursuit can stay cheaply tracked. A stranger can start an independent pursuit without having met the player. |
| Affect the city or world | Require a concrete outcome through an existing owner, such as accepting an office, forming a durable agreement, or changing available stock. Prominence depends on what the person can do and achieves. |
| Temporarily increase detail | Use more detailed dialogue, decisions or actions when a person is relevant to the current scene or conflict. End that extra work when it is no longer needed. The existing presence resolver still determines where they can be encountered. |

### Retention and lifecycle

For the first pilot, keep a bounded pool of recent, lightly acquainted people eligible to return, keyed by locality and subject to plausible presence. Keep a small encounter summary in that pool through a short save/load and return loop. Repeated meetings should build familiarity. A single consequential encounter can retain a person immediately, for example through a promise, a witnessed event with lasting effects, or an ongoing activity. Personal ties, shared history, appointments and offscreen importance can all make retention durable. Exact window and thresholds are pilot choices.

Temporary recollection can fade, and small talk and old scene detail can be compacted. Before discarding a temporary record, check that it has no durable personal facts relied on by other records and no unresolved references, claims, ownership, journeys, affiliations, or office. Once a fact is consequential, preserve the stable identity and established facts that bear on it, including promises, committed changes, witnessed acts, and death with its date and cause. Preserve who learned or reported disputed information when later actions depend on it. A compact summary or historical record can suffice. If an established identity returns, maintain its known name, characteristics, relationships and meeting history. Fill incidental gaps only where they are compatible with known history. Never backfill a consequential deed, witness, or transfer as if the simulation had already established it.

| Lifecycle operation | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| Reduce simulation detail | Release expensive decisions or scene state while keeping the person alive, their UID, and the facts and commitments needed to resume. Advance age and settle meaningful deadlines, including lifespan expiry, on the shared calendar. Use coarse catch-up where it preserves the existing rules. |
| Discard transient encounter data | Remove unreferenced scene-only data or let incidental familiarity expire under the pilot policy. Record no death. A scene refresh alone leaves background resident counts unchanged. |
| Preserve consequential identity and history | Retain a compact live or historical record whenever an obligation, relationship, event, ownership or other durable reference depends on it. |
| Resolve genuine death | Lifespan, combat or a world event can end a life through the lifecycle owner. Record the cause and date, resolve dependent claims once, and keep the UID wherever history refers to it. The simulation budget must never determine that someone died. Old cap-marked rows lack reliable evidence for automatic revival. |

### Population and accounting

Use existing city conditions to select plausible backgrounds and opportunities. A persistent shortage can create work for carriers or an opening for a competing supplier. A successful bid can then change actual procurement, which changes the conditions that later residents encounter. A recurring abstract development can produce a UID-backed person or an identifiable institution when an outcome needs one. Use the same rules while the player is present or absent.

- Where actual headcounts exist, an encounter that draws on a background resident can use a temporary reference within the band. When tracked as a named resident outside that band, reserve and debit one resident and establish their UID in the same transition. Apply later residence changes once. Reabsorbing a living resident into a band requires an established origin and residence and credits it once. A visitor influences local decisions through presence without increasing the resident total.
- Where population is only a rough baseline, use it as context. The visible roster has no fixed sampling ratio.
- When an abstract development gains a person or institution, carry forward its existing outcome or charter reference, credit for achievements, and accounted influence. Continue from that recorded state. A later named actor must not receive a second award or increase institutional influence again merely because the story acquired a face.
- Use existing finite-stock transfers for actual acquisitions, thefts, or deliveries and derive settlement changes from their committed results. An aggregate supply band can affect a forecast. A proposal or charter can precede an operational institution. Record real stock and roster commitments at the point of operation, once.
- Use a durable key for materialization and each committed consequence, scoped to its source and period. Reuse receipts or outcome IDs where possible. Keep initial updates reversible or safe to repeat because scheduler quarantine preserves failures and cannot roll back partly applied effects. ### City evolution and political pressure

A city normally maintains a recognizable order. The city lord, and a council where established, can defend existing arrangements or pursue reform. Independent people and groups can seek profit, change policy, gain standing, break laws, or challenge authority, including through rebellion. Represent the pressures as concrete objectives, evidence, commitments and constraints that can develop at different speeds. A supply shortage, a succession dispute and a campaign for reform can share the same review cycle while affecting different decisions and outcomes. An overall stability description can be derived where useful. The underlying conflicts retain their own causes and stakes.

Start with one Redwell case tied to an existing delivery or discovered loss. Give the incumbent one local objective, such as reliable herb access, and one challenger a separate objective, such as an independent association gaining a procurement contract. A challenger can be an existing lodge, merchant collective, reformer, rival cultivator, crime ring, or new identifiable group. Record what they want, what condition would satisfy them, and the next review date. Reuse that small shape across future ambitions. Each case defines its own evidence, feasible actions and effects using the relevant owners. A procurement rival needs delivery records. A reformer might need a petition and council support. The first case only needs rules for its own pressure and response.

At a scheduled review, assess recent evidence and the feasible responses for this government. A lord might negotiate a limited supply contract, tolerate an independent market, bring the challenger into the existing institution, or suppress an actual threat. A council, if the city has one, can constrain which response is feasible or require a different threshold. Existing ties, standing, stock, and prior commitments should matter. Redwell can keep existing authored constraints, while a city with a different charter can expose different responses.

A single failed shipment should change confidence or start a review. Sustained failures over several reviews can produce a contract change, an enduring local shortage, or an institutional change with a named cause. Losing an incumbent should change the holder and reopen only commitments that depended on that person. Established sects, dependable supply, protected offices, and strong cultivators limit the scope of change. Use tenure, repeated evidence, cooldowns, and materially different thresholds for escalation and recovery. Routine reviews should often leave the status quo in place.

Use existing realm bands and established assets as feasibility inputs. A clearly superior cultivator can make direct suppression implausible regardless of headcount. That still leaves room for a weaker group to pursue trade, patronage, or concessions. A city should never use player-relative NPC duel stats as autonomous capability. Keep those encounter rules unchanged. Treat independently produced high-realm cultivators as uncommon, evidence-based developments under the existing hierarchy. Reserve changes to their advancement probabilities for a separate design decision. ## Calendar, persistence, and migration

- **Within a life.** Persist only facts and decisions necessary to continue. These include the settlement key, evaluation date, active objectives, obligations, material outcome/receipt IDs, office references, consequential population bands where used, world NPCs, and unresolved world deadlines. Keep bounded summaries for display and reserve durable tombstones for referenced people. Rebuild spotlight and other runtime indexes. Maintain the existing migration rollback and save-failure protections.
- **Across lives.** Cross-life world continuity is a core requirement. Current reincarnation resets the world. Future lives must eventually be able to discover and encounter the accumulated consequences of earlier lives. Keep stable world identities, committed outcomes and deadlines on the shared calendar from the first pilot. Keep their ownership explicit wherever they currently reside in `G`, so a continuation can retain world consequences independently of old-player inventory, affinities, quest ownership and timers. Preserve the world date and set the new character’s birth date relative to it, as succession already separates age from calendar. The save layout, exact gap, pending commitments and transfer rules need a separate design review. Build on the existing run-end journal, carry receipt and single-writer protection. Keep the ended world recoverable until the continuation and new player save can be recovered, including failure and retry at each write or deletion. Verify once-only awards, consequences and time advancement, and preserve old saves. A same-life time skip can demonstrate the first historical consequence before this persistence work. [Single-writer protection](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/pull/255) · [Continuing-world direction](../ideas/procedural-zone-sect-ecology.md#persistent-world-owner-north-star)
- **Old saves.** Seed optional settlement state at the save’s current date from its authored profile and existing ledgers. A save without city history has no reliable record of earlier developments. Do not simulate missing centuries or infer exact city population from a visible roster. Preserve generated sites, seat names and birth dates, NPC UIDs, current affiliations, existing stock, and queued events. Attach a civic holder UID only when identity is established. Preserve ambiguous history and the incomplete-successor marker from #249. Avoid deleting old ambient tombstones until reference ownership can be audited. Migration should be idempotent and fail closed on corrupt data.
- **Chronology.** Use coarse steps anchored to calendar periods. For example, a pilot could review Redwell seasonally and require several consistent reviews to change a contract. Recheck meaningful dated developments through the existing scheduler. Process all causal boundaries before dependent updates. For a long jump with no relevant change, batch homogeneous periods as an optimization only after proving equivalence to shorter steps. Use stable per-city and per-period randomness if a review needs it. Independent event IDs and saved checkpoints should make save/load and tick partition irrelevant to that city outcome. There is no reason for the world to advance while the browser is closed or while choosing a new character unless design explicitly sets a time lapse.

## Performance and validation

The [prior audit’s synthetic benchmark](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md#simulation-scalability) found that 250, 2,000, and 10,000 added dead records yielded about 42k, 296k, and 1.46M whole-fixture JSON characters. Its paired NPC scans over 120 invocations took about 8, 58, and 284 ms. Those are Node VM stress results. They establish a cost trend without showing ordinary campaign frame times, browser write latency, or a need to change engines.

Expected city cost follows the number of participating settlements and consequential deadlines, not their resident headcount. Detailed decisions follow the bounded spotlight/active set. World records still incur save and lookup costs even when dormant. Initially reuse the journey index and existing scheduler. Keep only active development records in the evaluation set where possible. Before adding indexes, retention machinery, or workers, measure:

1. Visible encounters versus durable actors and unreferenced tombstones across a normal session and a deliberately long seclusion.
2. Active actors, pending events, evaluations per simulated century, roster scans, save bytes, serialization time, and storage write latency.
3. Frame time in a representative browser while viewing Redwell and while advancing a distant settlement. Include median and worst-window measurements and compare them against current main.
4. State and receipt equality for stepped versus batched simulation, repeated load, changes while absent, and a replayed scheduled event. A capture from an actual campaign matters more than running larger synthetic populations alone.

In the original review, eight existing suites passed on the audited baseline: unified disciple identity, NPC travel, time/NPC consistency, reincarnation safety, resource sources, organisation foundations, NPC action/reaction, and procedural lesser sects. The cap reproduction reused the existing in-memory identity harness. No browser performance profile or city simulation prototype was run.

## Implementation order for review

1. **Successor safety.** The first and urgent correctness fix shipped in #254. Keep its full-cap and reload guarantees when revisiting the lifecycle. The guard protects the active heir. Eliminating budget-driven death for other actors remains part of the lifecycle work.
2. **Minimal encounter-to-identity pilot.** Use current Redwell scenes. Exercise meeting an ordinary person, leaving and returning within the recent-encounter window, building familiarity without a quest, and retaining the same UID through crowd refresh, budget pressure and save/load once the person matters. Cover immediate retention for a consequential encounter and safe expiration for an unreferenced scene record. Verify that reducing detail keeps living actors alive and that genuine death still settles relevant claims. Measure record counts and save size before widening the scope.
3. **One offscreen Redwell development.** Review a real delivery or discovered loss through an existing office, and give the player a way to see one durable local consequence on return. Check presence and absence, reload, and stepped and batched time. Keep existing stock owners. Test that a report reveals only what its source could know.
4. **Extend only where a second case warrants it.** If an outcome needs a new identifiable person or institution, add one explicit materialization and accounting receipt. Address the audit’s transfer findings before adding new writers of finite stock. Agree on player-independent capability before resolving player-absent contests with lasting consequences. [Post-stabilization audit](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md)
5. **Implement cross-life continuation separately.** Review the persistence and recovery contract before changing reincarnation. Verify preservation of the ended world, creation of the next life, the existing single-writer boundary and carry receipt, and completion without duplicate history, stock or elapsed time.

## Decisions before implementation

| Before | Settle | Can wait |
|---|---|---|
| Encounter pilot | Choose what immediately makes identity durable, how ordinary repeated contact earns familiarity, and the scope of the recent-encounter pool. Define which established facts and references survive compaction and what to do with ambiguous older records. | General dormancy machinery, population bands, and a universal social-memory model. |
| Redwell pilot | Choose one cause, the incumbent and challenger’s objectives, the allowed response under Redwell’s existing authority, and what the returning player can discover. Identify any stock or claims involved and their owners. | Government replacement, city-wide political simulation, additional kinds of government, broader market dynamics, and generalized NPC decision-making. |
| Cross-life continuation | Decide the gap and treatment of bitter and true reincarnation, which commitments follow the prior person or remain with their office, and which state the next life can inherit. Establish recovery and migration before changing the current run-end transition. | The continuation save format can wait through the first pilots. Stable IDs, shared calendar dates, durable outcomes and clear world ownership guide those pilots now. |

The first stopping point is a familiar Redwell face whose identity survives the loss of scene detail. The second is one understandable local change that happens while the player is away, survives save/load, and can be discovered on return. Each is a proposal for review.
