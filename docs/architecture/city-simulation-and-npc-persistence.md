# City simulation and NPC persistence

**Status** — Proposal for review. Baseline is [merged main at `6fcbb73`](https://github.com/WanderingImmortal/tales-immortal-path/commit/6fcbb731af096a642f19124d36909994cf1f4c67), including the [post-stabilization audit](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md). This proposal authorizes no implementation or balance changes.

## Recommendation

Use settlements as the owners of slow local developments. Keep people, organisations, offices, and physical inventories under their existing owners. A city determines which local changes plausibly happen. A named person retains their own identity and accounts, wherever they go.

Start with two independent changes for review. First, protect the active successor from population retirement. Then prototype a single Redwell development on the existing calendar, with a durable cause and consequence. Defer a full economy, general-purpose agent AI, and cross-life migration until the prototype shows which data is actually needed. If the existing office and incident systems can support the pilot with a compact settlement record, a separate city simulator framework would add little value.

### What the cap actually does

`enforcePersistentCap()` compares a **global** threshold of 100 to the subset of living persisted rows with `state === 'persistent'`, a meeting timestamp, and no current player-sect affiliation. It orders them by low impression, then low talk count. It marks a selected actor dead if they have fewer than two conversations, and stops at the first selected actor that fails that check. Thus it is neither a hard bound on all durable NPCs nor a budget for detailed activity. It does not remove the record or call the normal death path. The separate per-zone ambient cap is 10, and scene refresh can also mark old crowd rows dead directly. [data.js](../../data.js#L3351) · [ambient-npcs.js](../../ambient-npcs.js#L175) · [npc-presence.js](../../npc-presence.js#L58)

I reproduced the successor failure on this baseline by filling the cap’s eligible subset after installing a successor with one conversation and low impression. The cap marked the successor dead. Living UID lookup then failed while both the player game-over and succession flags were false. This is an identity defect independent of any city design choice. A journey, office, durable relationship, or unresolved obligation can also make an actor consequential even when a talk count is low. Which other current actors can actually reach the retirement branch depends on their initialization and affiliations. ## Proposed model

### Settlement state and authority

Use the existing settlement location ID as the key. A settlement can include an authored hinterland with links to its contributing sites. A zone may contain multiple cities, outposts, wild sites, sects, and mobile groups. Treating every location or an entire zone as one city would attribute their actors and stock incorrectly. A wilderness source or a roaming cultivator may have no city owner.

Begin with a small settlement state under the existing world root. Store its last evaluated calendar period, one durable development, and only the aggregate or policy inputs that this development needs. Keep starting conditions, capability limits, and allowed institutions in the settlement profile. Read market conditions, offices, and known regional shocks from their owning systems. Add coarse population or cultivation bands when a concrete development requires them. Prosperity, order, and effectiveness can begin as derived observations. They need independent stored meters only if play requires distinct causes and responses.

| Owner | Proposed authority |
|---|---|
| Calendar | Existing `G.ageMonths` and scheduled events. A settlement’s evaluation date is a checkpoint on the shared calendar. |
| Settlement | Local conditions, aggregate background population where modeled, durable policy or pressure records, and a bounded public consequence history. |
| Person | One NPC UID for mutable identity, progression, runtime location, and personal history. Player control uses the same successor identity. |
| Office or organisation | Its institutional identity, policies, and membership or occupant references. Existing named civic holders can remain embedded until a feature requires an NPC UID. Once promoted, the seat retains the UID and adapters derive copies for old readers. |
| Stocks and claims | Existing resource sources, inventories and procurement ledgers own finite amounts. Existing travel, Hall, mission and situation records own their commitments. City state refers to these records. |

City summaries should be read-only. Initialization and evaluation should have explicit simulation entry points, so a city does not advance faster because the player visits its panel more often.

### Population, accounting, and importance

Treat **identity retention**, **simulation detail**, and **physical presence** independently. A persistent elder may be distant and dormant, an ambient stranger may receive a temporary scene spotlight, and a city lord may matter offscreen while receiving only deadline-based updates. Travel availability remains the presence resolver’s decision.

Use a clear boundary between the abstract and named contributions:

- A city’s abstract resident bands represent unmodeled residents. For a named resident explicitly created from a band, reserve and debit one resident of that band and create its UID in the same settlement transition. If that actor later leaves, the city records the appropriate residence change once. A passing visitor can influence a specific decision through their actual presence without permanently increasing resident totals.
- If a pilot needs only a rough baseline and has no headcounts, treat it as context and do not add the visible roster on top to infer population. A scene’s handful of people has no reliable sampling ratio.
- For individual stock uses, apply the existing transfer once and derive any settlement consequence from the committed result. Aggregate supply bands can alter an abstract forecast. They must not independently award the same stock, roll the same mission, or subtract the same theft. A generated institution may first appear as a proposal or charter. Make physical resource and roster commitments only when it becomes operational.
- Each materialization, completed city development, or cross-system consequence needs a durable deduplication key scoped to its source and calendar period. Reuse existing receipt or outcome patterns. Restrict the first pilot to reversible or idempotent updates because scheduler quarantine does not roll back a partially applied handler. ### NPC lifecycle

| Step | Rule to test before extending it |
|---|---|
| Generate | Use anonymous scene actors for short-lived flavor and aggregate residents for background. Only materialize a durable UID for a meaningful commitment, relationship, appointment, or independently generated development. Author control remains available for story actors. |
| Promote or spotlight | Preserve the actor’s UID when a scene person becomes durable. Player interaction can qualify them, and an offscreen accomplishment, office, or institution can give them the same protection. Spotlight adds work to an active set and can end without affecting age, life status, or references. |
| Dormancy | Keep UID, age basis, cultivation, residence or journey, obligations, and retained history in the authoritative record. Do cheap calendar catch-up or scheduled deadline work where it preserves the existing result. Resolve lifespan expiry and other consequential deadlines even while distant. Keep current cultivation rules until a separate pacing review establishes that a batched equivalent is safe. |
| Retire simulation detail | Release scene detail from unimportant actors first. Only discard a temporary, unreferenced actor when all owners agree there is no durable history, obligation, ownership, quest, active journey, affiliation, seat, or other consequential reference. Discarding scene-only detail leaves resident counts unchanged. Reabsorbing a previously counted living resident would credit the corresponding band once and requires a known origin and residence. |
| Genuine death | Record cause and date via the existing lifecycle and resolve dependent claims once. Keep a historical UID where referenced. Death and retirement are distinct outcomes. Old cap-marked rows lack reliable evidence for automatic revival. |

**Immediate successor guard.** Exclude the controlled leader before ranking retirement candidates, using `isPlayerControlledNpc()` and the current `leaderNpcUid`. Assert that activity, presence, and track alias survive a cap pass and save/load. Check that eligible transient actors can still leave the active population and that former leaders still follow genuine death and succession. Protect active journeys and consequential references before expanding retirement beyond this narrow fix. Counting a protected actor against a detail budget is fine if only their detail can be reduced. Their life and record stay intact.

### City evolution and political pressure

Start with one Redwell case tied to an existing delivery or discovered loss. Give the incumbent one local objective, such as reliable herb access, and one challenger a separate objective, such as an independent association gaining a procurement contract. A challenger can be an existing lodge, merchant collective, reformer, rival cultivator, crime ring, or new identifiable group. Record what they want, what condition would satisfy them, and the next review date. Their objective is what an outcome changes.

At a scheduled review, assess recent evidence and the feasible responses for this government. A lord might negotiate a limited supply contract, tolerate an independent market, bring the challenger into the existing institution, or suppress an actual threat. A council, if the city has one, can constrain which response is feasible or require a different threshold. Existing ties, standing, stock, and prior commitments should matter. Redwell can keep existing authored constraints, while a city with a different charter can expose different responses.

A single failed shipment should change confidence or start a review. Sustained failures over several reviews can produce a contract change, an enduring local shortage, or an institutional change with a named cause. Losing an incumbent should change the holder and reopen only commitments that depended on that person. Established sects, dependable supply, protected offices, and strong cultivators limit the scope of change. Use tenure, repeated evidence, cooldowns, and materially different thresholds for escalation and recovery. Routine reviews should often leave the status quo in place.

Use existing realm bands and established assets as feasibility inputs. A clearly superior cultivator can make direct suppression implausible regardless of headcount. That still leaves room for a weaker group to pursue trade, patronage, or concessions. A city should never use player-relative NPC duel stats as autonomous capability. Keep those encounter rules unchanged. Treat independently produced high-realm cultivators as uncommon, evidence-based developments under the existing hierarchy. Reserve changes to their advancement probabilities for a separate design decision. ## Calendar, persistence, and migration

- **Within a life.** Persist only facts and decisions necessary to continue. These include the settlement key, evaluation date, active objectives, obligations, material outcome/receipt IDs, office references, consequential population bands where used, world NPCs, and unresolved world deadlines. Keep bounded summaries for display and reserve durable tombstones for referenced people. Rebuild spotlight and other runtime indexes. Maintain the existing migration rollback and save-failure protections.
- **Across lives.** Treat world continuity as a separate feature with a reviewed ownership map. Continue the old world date and place the new player’s birth date relative to it, as succession already separates age from calendar. Decide explicitly which facts are world-owned before retaining them. City state, consequential NPCs, ownership, stock, and active commitments are candidates. Player inventory, affinity scores, quest ownership, and still-player-bound timers require field-by-field decisions. Do not copy the full runtime into the legacy chronicle. Preserve the ended world until its continuation and the new player save can be recovered. Leverage the existing run-end journal and carry receipt. If a continuation uses a separate stored record, define a single recoverable publication point and test failure between every write and deletion. Resolve the stale-writer safeguard from A-04 before depending on safe retention of the prior world. For a pilot, use a same-life descendant or time skip to demonstrate historical change without changing reincarnation.
- **Old saves.** Seed optional settlement state at the save’s current date from its authored profile and existing ledgers. A save without city history has no reliable record of earlier developments. Do not simulate missing centuries or infer exact city population from a visible roster. Preserve generated sites, seat names and birth dates, NPC UIDs, current affiliations, existing stock, and queued events. Attach a civic holder UID only when identity is established. Preserve ambiguous history and the incomplete-successor marker from #249. Avoid deleting old ambient tombstones until reference ownership can be audited. Migration should be idempotent and fail closed on corrupt data.
- **Chronology.** Use coarse steps anchored to calendar periods. For example, a pilot could review Redwell seasonally and require several consistent reviews to change a contract. Recheck meaningful dated developments through the existing scheduler. Process all causal boundaries before dependent updates. For a long jump with no relevant change, batch homogeneous periods as an optimization only after proving equivalence to shorter steps. Use stable per-city and per-period randomness if a review needs it. Independent event IDs and saved checkpoints should make save/load and tick partition irrelevant to that city outcome. There is no reason for the world to advance while the browser is closed or while choosing a new character unless design explicitly sets a time lapse.

## Performance and validation

The [prior audit’s synthetic benchmark](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md#simulation-scalability) found that 250, 2,000, and 10,000 added dead records yielded about 42k, 296k, and 1.46M whole-fixture JSON characters. Its paired NPC scans over 120 invocations took about 8, 58, and 284 ms. Those are Node VM stress results. They establish a cost trend without showing ordinary campaign frame times, browser write latency, or a need to change engines.

Expected city cost follows the number of participating settlements and consequential deadlines, not their resident headcount. Detailed decisions follow the bounded spotlight/active set. World records still incur save and lookup costs even when dormant. Initially reuse the journey index and existing scheduler. Keep only active development records in the evaluation set where possible. Before adding indexes, retention machinery, or workers, measure:

1. Visible encounters versus durable actors and unreferenced tombstones across a normal session and a deliberately long seclusion.
2. Active actors, pending events, evaluations per simulated century, roster scans, save bytes, serialization time, and storage write latency.
3. Frame time in a representative browser while viewing Redwell and while advancing a distant settlement. Include median and worst-window measurements and compare them against current main.
4. State and receipt equality for stepped versus batched simulation, repeated load, changes while absent, and a replayed scheduled event. A capture from an actual campaign matters more than running larger synthetic populations alone.

For this proposal, eight existing suites passed on the baseline: unified disciple identity, NPC travel, time/NPC consistency, reincarnation safety, resource sources, organisation foundations, NPC action/reaction, and procedural lesser sects. The cap reproduction reused the existing in-memory identity harness. No browser performance profile or city simulation prototype was run.

## Implementation order for review

1. **Successor safety.** Fix retirement eligibility and test a low-talk active heir at a full cap. Keep this independent of the proposal’s city design.
2. **Identity and detail pilot.** Separate transient scene cleanup from durable NPC lifetime in the crowd path. Confirm UID retention for a promoted actor, a travelling actor, and a historical reference. This can be tested with current Redwell scenes. Expand dormancy only when there is a measured detail need.
3. **One offscreen Redwell development.** Use an existing office and a recorded local pressure to produce a durable, conservative outcome. Verify that the city changes the same way when observed, when absent, after reload, and under stepped or batched time. Keep the initial outcome small and avoid new settlement-wide stock writers.
4. **Aggregate-to-individual pilot.** Add one bounded new institution or person from that outcome, with a clear origin and one accounting receipt. Before sharing finite stock or adding autonomous training, resolve the [audit’s manual and transfer accounting findings](../audits/post-stabilization-audit-250.md). Before autonomous domain outcomes use cultivation capability, agree on a player-independent input and leave current encounter balance alone.
5. **Continuity across lives.** After the city ownership map and pilot have proven useful, review a continuation save design. Test old-world retention, new-life commit, stale writers, and the existing reward receipt without duplicate history, stock, or elapsed time. Add other settlements individually based on their needs.

## Decisions to review

- Should both bitter and true reincarnation preserve the world? Should a new life start immediately, after an authored gap, or under a selectable rule?
- Which commitments are personal to a prior player and which stick to an office or institution? What happens to the player-controlled actor’s obligations at death, voluntary reincarnation, or departure?
- At what point should a memorable encounter create a durable identity, even with no ongoing relationship? What traces should a former city lord leave if they had only a lightweight office profile?
- Which Redwell decisions should be possible under an unchanged ruler, and what evidence should justify replacing the government? Which constraints should stay authored or different for a sanctuary, a frontier town, and a sect seat?
- Does the first prototype need population bands at all? If action comes from one blocked delivery or vacant office, the event and policy may explain enough. Add a band only where it helps players understand or influence the city.
- Which stocks should real city actions compete for, and which continue as abstract supply? Which cultivation capabilities matter for deterrence, negotiation, and actual conflict?
- How much city history should be discoverable through scenes and records, and when should a consequence be important enough to enter the shared chronicle?

The proposed stopping point is one settlement changing for an understandable reason while the player is away, with its consequences surviving save/load and consequential NPCs staying resolvable. That can be evaluated before increasing the number of cities, simulation detail, or supported government forms.
