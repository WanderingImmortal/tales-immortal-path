## 1. Why the Tian still rule (owner 2026-09-26)

**Problem:** the Tian deterrent is a sleeping **Half-Step**; the Sword Ancestor later ascends to **Immortal**, other sects gain Immortals, and the cult founder may be one too. The throne's trump card is outranked.

**Owner answer:**

- There is a **long gap** between the first Half-Step and later Immortals — generous, no dates needed yet.
- During that gap the **Tian and rising noble houses centralised power and wealth**. The **top end is restrained** (rough parity between sects and the imperial clan); the **middle** grew powerful enough that overthrowing the order is a serious challenge.
- Ancient grudges, new treaties, alliances — a mess. Stomping out any single great power gets harder. Most great powers **acquiesce on the surface** and **claim authority in their own area**.
- **Noble houses** are survivors of the **Warring States era**, then through the **Dao Wars** (owner notes). **One** house could be a **puppet** — not all.
- The Tian founder is the **first Half-Step in living memory** — not first ever.

**Suggestions that fit:**

| Point | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| First mover writes the paperwork | In the gap the Tian had the only unanswerable power, so the charter, Registry, Longcheng market, examinations and **vein leases** came from them. |
| The middle is chained to the throne | Many house fortunes rest on Tian-issued deeds. Topple the throne and every deed is up for grabs. *They don't love the throne; they love their deeds.* |
| Immortals cancel out | An Immortal moving openly invites heaven's resistance and rival Immortals' answer — decisive strength is **Void Refinement and below**. |
| Authority by area | Tian rule the Heartlands core + Longcheng; great sects run homelands under charter recognition; outer zones get tribute fiction (fits Dustbone's weak reach). |
| Nine-realm note | Noble houses' hidden **Void Refinement** ancestors (idx 5) are insurance, **not** gate candidates — the gate sits above **Dao Manifestation** (idx 7). |

**Puppet house — whose?** (open) Tian front · a great sect's vote in the capital · someone off the board (grotto master / Heavenly Demon Cult; patriarch may not know).

**Tension sources:** Tian is not one bloc (regents, blood branches, cadet lines); a noble house outgrowing a cadet Tian branch; nobody can prove the sleeping founder can still wake.

## 2. Heaven's cycle — why a permanent cost is too much

**Owner:** heaven is **not a feeling entity** — a set of rules for the universe's shape. It **prefers** no Immortals rather than holding a hard cap. It resists beings that **take resources essentially permanently** and **keep growing** and requiring more.

**Rejected:** a hard **"gate budget"** (fixed number of Immortal slots). New Immortals must be able to ascend and change the world.

**Working frame (suggestion, built on by owner):** heaven's law is a **cycle**. Everything borrows; **death repays the loan** — qi, flesh and soul return through the veins, the land and reincarnation. A 50,000-year Dao Manifestation lifespan is fine because it **ends**. An apex being is a loan that is never repaid.

- **What is too much?** Not a size — **any cost that never comes back**.
- **Resistance, not a ceiling:** heaven's pushback scales with how much is held out of the cycle versus what remains. A richer world = less resistance at the gate.
- **Immortals as dams:** they don't strip life force from people. World qi is meant to circulate; each Immortal holds a growing share out of it. Downstream slowly runs dry.
- **Why the founders allowed it:** the Seal needed a few beings who would never die to guard and maintain it — a deliberate exception for a handful. The rule "assumed sparse ascenders." The **Void Seeker** is the exception working as intended.

### Who knows

| Who | Knowledge |
|-----|-----------|
| **Mortals → Dao Manifestation** | It never occurs to them. **Owner:** DM-equivalents notice the world getting worse over the ages but can't figure out why. |
| **Immortals (long-lived)** | **Owner:** discover clues over time — possibly via **tribulation**; they feel the world doesn't want them to exist. *Suggestion:* post-ascension pressure never stops — like a body rejecting a transplant or pushing out a splinter; Immortal Calamities feel like being expelled, not tested. |
| **Know exactly (owner)** | **Void Seeker**, **Tian founder**, possibly the **Heavenly Demon**. It shapes their actions (guard · sleep · devour). *Why they don't tell — later.* |

**Red herring (suggestion):** elders blame the **Dao War law scars**, over-harvested veins and fading lineages — partly true, so nobody looks upward.

### How it shows up

**Owner lean:** decline over **many centuries or millennia**; world qi slowly slipping away, the world growing harsher. Must be managed carefully so it isn't punishing. Existing meter: **World Fortune** in [`post-immortal-cosmology.md`](post-immortal-cosmology.md) (starts ~85, slow drift, floor ~20 fires "mortal age" events).

**Suggestions for shape:**

1. **Uneven, not uniform** — held qi pools near Immortals; homelands grow lush, distant zones thin. Sects want Immortals because the cost is exported.
2. **Lowers the ceiling, not the player's speed** — fewer prodigies, rarer high-grade roots, veins ageing, rare herbs scarcer. Mostly hits NPC population and rare resources.
3. **Seen on return** — seclusion skips show a harsher world after centuries.
4. **A tide, not a slide** — recovery when Immortals fall; good eras and bad eras.
5. **Hidden number until the reveal** — signs only before (elder dialogue, sect droughts).

### Immortal death — "the last dying gift"

**Owner:** an Immortal's death floods the world with qi. Non-immortals see it as the **last dying gift** to the world; Immortals know it is repayment. **No Immortal death is recorded yet.** *Suggestion:* the first recorded one is a world-shaking event — atonement, assassination, or the player's doing.

### Returning / raising world qi

**Owner lean:** yes — a way to **restrain oneself** as an Immortal while still acting in the world a little, and to **allow more Immortals** to be born.

| Layer | Suggestion |
|-------|------------|
| **Voluntary release** | An Immortal's held-qi pool; acting draws more, releasing gives back at a personal cost (weaker power, slower pace, a little mortality). Void Seeker is the model. |
| **Healing the land** | Formations, vein nurture, root rites **repair and reroute** qi — they don't create it. Sect / noble investment. |
| **Death** | The big return (above). |
| **Self-sustaining apexes** | True Immortal, Personal God — little or no world cost. |
| **Politics** | Restoring qi lowers gate resistance for **everyone** — healing the land helps rivals ascend. |

**Guardrail:** returning qi always costs the giver; world recovery is slow — no free "act, then refund."

## 3. The three apexes

Lane words (from PR #127): qi → 仙 **Immortal** · body → 圣 **Saint** · spirit → 神 **God**. Suggestion: the three run **parallel**, each withholding a different part of the cycle.

| Apex | What it keeps from the cycle | Heaven's resistance | Why it's rare |
|------|------------------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Immortal** (qi) | World qi, held in the self | Grows with time and use | Growing world cost; gate pressure |
| **Saint** (body) | Flesh that never returns to the earth | Hard at each crossing, mild between | Material cost; the crossing itself |
| **God** (spirit) | A soul outside heaven's judgment | Heaven contests the soul directly | Surviving the reclaiming |

### Saints (body)

- **Owner:** body takes little from the world beyond **materials** — either something in sanctifying goes against what heaven allows, or the sheer resource cost gates most people.
- **Owner:** the cost **must still grow** — the body has to keep progressing or it's a dead end for players. Hard breakthrough then little resistance is a neat feel.
- **Suggestion — cost grows with growth, not time:** each further refinement pulls in more material and locks more substance into non-returning flesh, and meets heaven's resistance again (a smaller crossing). A Saint who stops growing stays cheap.
- Fits existing lore: Da Chi's flesh "won't settle"; body power is "excavated from inside the body."

**Walking-bomb problem (owner):** low audit means Saints can make more moves. Only the Mad Arhat exists today — excusable by mutual destruction or disinterest — but new Saints could be uncontrollable without extreme risk. **Suggested containment:**

1. **Visible approach** — nobody hoards enough cores, blood and ore quietly; charter hoarding clauses, courting, containment, pre-breakthrough assassination. Explains why there's only one. *Story hook:* how did Da Chi break through after capitulation unnoticed?
2. **Short reach** — devastating up close, no regional press (Celestial Avatar) or Immortal reach; grand arrays can pin; ranged powers keep distance.
3. **Vessel Rules** — the Saint's own oaths bind harder than heaven (Da Chi doesn't walk away from the line). A Saint with no rules is the real nightmare.
4. Sacrilege / corruption tracking still applies to atrocities.

### Gods (spirit)

- **Owner:** Gods interact with their souls at a fundamental level to sustain them and keep them **out of the reincarnation loop heaven adjudicates** — which is why divinity is so hard.
- **Suggestion:** the soul is what heaven **judges** (karma, oaths, rebirth). A God's conflict is **authority**, not cost. The deification trial = heaven trying to **reclaim the soul while you live** (karma read at once, heart demons).
- **Void Temple tension:** natural spirit-path home **and** guardian of the soul cycle.
- *Optional hook:* the **Void Prisoner** (reincarnation tampering) as what heaven fears a God becomes.

**Three kinds of God:**

| Kind | Sustained by | Note |
|------|--------------|------|
| **Worshipped God** | Freely given faith | Celestial Avatar echo → shrine. Open: is worship truly clean? |
| **Devouring God** | Other souls | Forbidden branch — [`soul-body-refining.md`](soul-body-refining.md) |
| **Personal God** *(owner)* | Self alone | Godhood for your own sake; **reclaiming your ego** to yourself and only your authority; no worship, no reaping. Costs the world almost nothing; hardest of all. Heaven sees you but has no right to judge — **not** grotto severance (being forgotten). |

**Soul idx 5 (owner, hazy):** *Selfless Refinement* may need a rename — or become **merging yourself with the world**, then later **reclaiming yourself with the world in you**. **Suggestion:** make idx 5 the dissolve point and the three God kinds three answers to "what now?" — held by others (Worshipped), take others in (Devouring), gather yourself back with the world inside (Personal). Name candidates: Boundless Soul · Self Dissolution · World Merging · keep Selfless Refinement for the irony. **Don't lock until the spirit path is designed.**

### Borrowed / stolen / self-made (suggestion)

| | Borrowed | Stolen | Self-made |
|--|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Qi** | Mandate Immortal (world qi) | Heavenly Demon (devouring) | True Immortal |
| **Spirit** | Worshipped God | Devouring God | Personal God |
| **Body** | Materials Saint? | Blood-refined flesh (cult)? | Closed-cycle Saint? |

**Self-made body (esoteric, optional):** the body becomes its **own closed cycle** — nothing enters, nothing leaves, everything recycles inside (body as a small heaven and earth). No breath, no hunger, wounds close from within, stone-still, faintly warm. Fine to leave empty.

## 4. Intent as the bridge to souls

- **Owner:** Intent's apex **Self-Will** gets a pass — intent is less ego, more **willpower manifest through a weapon**. Soul cultivators manifest will **directly**, no medium.
- **Owner idea:** at that level, the qi path could start **hurting souls** — a way for qi to fight soul cultivators.
- **Suggestion:** anyone can learn to wound souls **through intent** (sword intent that cuts the soul; body **Martial Intent** fists that shake the soul); only soul cultivators do it **without a medium**. Soul cultivators keep bypassing armour and flesh. Spirit doc already gives soul enemies "no flesh guard, deep soul guard." Home: [`combat-damage-depth.md`](combat-damage-depth.md).
