## Two beats — tide, then proof

| Beat | What happened | Who understood |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| **1 — The tide** | The **Tian founder** broke through to **Half-Step Immortal** *during* the siege — first soul on the continent to touch the gate. **Every** major camp felt the realm shift: arrays flickered, oaths tightened, old monsters woke. | Sages, array masters, peak elders — *something* ended. **Not yet** who won. |
| **2 — The garden** | The breakthrough **was not decisive** until the Half-Step **stepped onto the field** before **Tianjing’s ancient gates** — the Tian clan’s own city in the Dao Wars ([`imperial-city-tianjing.md`](imperial-city-tianjing.md)). ## The siege of Tianjing (天京)

**Tianjing** was already the **Tian clan’s city** — not yet wrapped in today’s **Longcheng** outer sprawl. The imperial metropolis **grew outward after** the mandate; charter Longcheng is layers of hub, sect branches, and noble quarters built on victory’s scaffolding. What the coalition assaulted was the **inner seat**: ancient walls, underpalace veins, and defenses **older and deeper** than anything the four sects openly admit to.

Rumors had run for years: the Tian bred a **genius** at **peak Dao Manifestation**, assailing Half-Step. **Spies** sat in every camp — but the only **great powers** who actually marched to **snuff the Tian** at the capital were:

| Belligerent | Role |
|-------------|------|
| **Celestial Sword** | Full martial commitment — **Sword Ancestor** (剑祖) era: the one who would become **Sword Immortal** (剑仙), **peak Dao Manifestation**, **wearing the law of the First Sword**. A **real peer threat** to the Tian founder **before** the breakthrough — **not** an Immortal yet. |
| **Golden Phoenix** | Largest flame column; **Flame Emperor** pushing **Realm Reforge** (#8). |
| **Heavenly Demon Cult** | **Harvest** on the siege — cauldrons, tagged formations; tolerated by coalition until the walk. |
| **Lesser clans & houses** | Many joined; most **ruined** — *parked: named survivors for later content* |

**Did not fight the Tian at the capital:**

| Power | Stance |
|-------|--------|
| **Jade Lotus** | **Would not** war against the Tian — squeezed by Phoenix/Sword elsewhere, consolidated valley, late **hedges**; after Half-Step, **Leng Shuyu’s bluff** → real Tian talks ([`jade-lotus-imperial-alliance-origin.md`](jade-lotus-imperial-alliance-origin.md)); **bloomed** after the walk ([`jade-lotus-sect.md`](jade-lotus-sect.md)). |
| **Void Temple** | **Silent** for most of the war — prison and seals first; **Archive Oath** only after outcome clear ([`void-temple-sect.md`](void-temple-sect.md)). |

**The two apexes (pre-walk):** Tian founder and **Sword Ancestor** both **peak Dao Manifestation** — the board’s ceiling **before** Half-Step existed. Chroniclers still argue whether the Sword Ancestor could have **killed** the genius **during** breakthrough if the inner defenses had failed one hour sooner.

**Tian defenses held one beat too long** — long enough for the genius to **finish** Half-Step **inside** Tianjing’s cordon. That hold was the work of **many** defenders under the elder brother's emergency patriarchy, not a solitary miracle. Tian myth: **dragon’s patience**. Coalition myth: **curse of overconfidence**.

## The garden walk — what everyone saw

The Half-Step emerged from Tianjing’s **inner keep / underpalace core** and **walked outward** through the gates. Grand formations **failed to constrain**. Sword and Phoenix battle-lines **broke** — not for lack of courage, but because **the category of force had changed**.

**Flame Emperor** **saw** the destruction at the gates. He **tested** the Half-Step’s power — a **brief, terrible exchange** — and **withdrew** rather than kneel, **injured** but alive. Phoenix inner court **does not deny** this; shame and pride **forbid** softening it into propaganda.

**Sword Ancestor** read the tide **before** the mountain spent him — informed in part by **prior encounters** with the Tian genius as opponents before the breakthrough (battles and outcomes deliberately vague; see [`celestial-sword-sect.md`](celestial-sword-sect.md) § Prior encounters). He **did not** duel the Half-Step to annihilation. He **receded** to Solitary Sword Mountain, raised the **Ten Thousand Swords Seal Array**, and sent envoys — judgement and loyalty to the mountain, not a panicked flight. The sect **openly accepts** their loss and their part in the assault — then **returns to the sword**.

| Targeted with particular ferocity | Why (Tian / orthodox memory) |
|-----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Heavenly Demon army** (cauldron legions, harvest arrays) | Worst cycle-tamperers; **fed on the siege**; Half-Step **hunted them first** in the walk — public proof the mandate was not “another sect” |
| **Demon-linked formations** | Blood pools that tried to **tag** the Half-Step’s wake — unmade on sight |

Cult inner court: the walk **shredded** the field army and the **Demon Blood Clone** (魔血分身) that wore the Demon’s face; the **true** founder was **not** in the line. Survivors ordered **Great Withdrawal** ([`heavenly-demon-cult.md`](heavenly-demon-cult.md)). Mandate claims **Heavenly Demon slain** here; juniors think it’s folklore. The Half-Step **likely** knew — and **did not** need to say so; Mo Xuan **suspects** as much, but **immortal standoff** today makes it moot.

## After Tianjing — Phoenix arc (owner merge v1)

The garden walk **did not** end open war the same afternoon. **Sword** was already receding; **Phoenix** still had an **injured** Flame Emperor and a realm that **refused** to kneel. What happened **next** depends on how you read the founder’s personality — three sketches, then the **merged** line we park in lore.

### Fork A — **The Seven Days of Weight** (七天垂压)

The Half-Step **stays in Tianjing** ~**seven days** without pursuing.

| In-world read | Fiction |
|---------------|---------|
| **Mandate myth** | The dragon **breathed** — letting the continent **feel** the new ceiling before passing judgment |
| **Phoenix read** | Cruel suspense — every hour Phoenix thought the march would start **now** |
| **Founder personality (contemplative)** | First soul to stand at the gate — **learning** a realm no one (they believe) has ever held; not partying, **measuring** heaven’s debt and qi pressure |
| **Founder personality (predatory)** | **Reveling** in tension — the week **is** the weapon; fear does half the work |

Then he **marches on Phoenix** — caldera approach or regrouped flame host.

### Fork B — **Immediate business** (趁热斩凰)

No pause — Half-Step **pursues** while coalition lines are **broken**. At the **next great battle site** (working name: **Ember Crown Plain** / 烬冠原 — where Phoenix masses for **Realm Reforge** (#8)), he **executes** the **Flame Emperor** in front of both armies.

| Purpose | Fiction |
|---------|---------|
| **Demoralize** | Phoenix **Sect Rebirth** (#7) breaks when the paragon dies **like a mortal criminal**, not in honorable duel ash |
| **Crown the self** | Mandate is born: *the board has one hand that can **kill** kings* |
| **Personality** | No theatre — **accounting** |

### Fork C — **Merged (canon)** — weight, then execution

Combines the **psychological week** with the **public execution** at the **second great site**:

```text
Tianjing garden walk
    → Sword recedes; demons Withdraw; Phoenix retreats injured
    → Seven Days of Weight (founder holds Tianjing; continent feels Half-Step)
    → Phoenix regroups at Ember Crown Plain — last Realm Reforge gamble
    → Tian march (deliberate, not rushed — business after the week)
    → Flame Emperor refuses to kneel; masses for last stand at Ember Crown / loses last stand
    → Half-Step EXECUTES (not duel) — demoralization; younger brother inherits ash
    → Open war ends when brother kneels; charter seasons follow
```

| Beat | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Why wait a week?** | Founder **consolidates** Half-Step *and* lets Phoenix **concentrate** — one strike breaks **hope**, not only soldiers |
| **Why execution?** | Flame Emperor already **tested** the gate at Tianjing and **refused to submit**; continued defiance at Ember Crown made **example** judgment necessary — not an honor duel. **No** documented ultimatum or formal negotiation during the Seven Days ([Ember Crown Plain — character, politics, settlement](#ember-crown-plain--character-politics-settlement-2026-11-07)). |
| **Sword distance** | Sword Ancestor **already** on the mountain — execution is **Phoenix–Tian** finale; Sword chronicles note it without glory |

**Founder personality (locked):** **contemplative dragon** — the **Younger brother** / imperial founding Immortal. The **Seven Days of Weight** are not theatre or cruelty for sport. Tianjing goes **quiet** (bells stopped, qi like pressure before rain). He **learns** what Half-Step **is**: heaven’s weight, debt on the senses, a realm they believe **no one** has stood in before. Chroniclers call it **stillness more terrifying than rage**. Later **sleeping dragon** myth reads the week as the last time he **fully measured** the gate before he chose **voluntary seclusion** rather than **rule by walking the world**. Public speech still says he **sleeps**; he is not literally unconscious.

**Ember Crown — Immortal-path technique (locked):** When the founder **arrived** at the plain, he did not fight like a peak Dao Manifestation with bigger numbers. He **wielded one art** — **Immortal Path** proper, or so close the continent had **no name** for it — the first public glimpse of **what lies past the gate**. Formation lines **did not break** so much as **fail to apply**; flame law **could not seat** on the board. The **Flame Emperor’s execution** is remembered as **one gesture of that art** — not a duel sequence, not a weapon showcase — **judgment** expressed in a technique mortals would later call *pre-immortal* or *half-heaven*. Phoenix records **do not** name the form cleanly; Void archives **redact** the syllables.

| Who remembers what | Detail |
|--------------------|--------|
| **Phoenix** | “He **arrived** with **heaven’s technique**”; execution indistinguishable from **end of Realm Reforge** hope |
| **Sword** | One line in the mountain annals: *the sword is a line; that day was a **plane*** |
| **Tian myth** | **Dragon’s Edict** (working name: 龙敕) — not taught; not in player manuals v1 |
| **Void** | Copyists argue whether it was **true** Immortal Path or **Half-Step** wearing one **borrowed** heaven stroke |

Phoenix inner court: **records all of it** — injury at Tianjing, seven days, arrival technique, execution — pride **forbids** denial ([`golden-phoenix-sect.md`](golden-phoenix-sect.md)).

### The Flame Emperor (焰帝) — leader, not fool

| Layer | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Canon** | Formidable cultivator and **expansionist** leader of Golden Phoenix / Phoenix Court during the Dao Wars. Proud, ambitious, committed to Phoenix Dao and sect standing. Pushed **Realm Reforge** (#8) — burn the stale continental board, not merely recover lost border courts. Among the **largest** wartime expansionists; Phoenix was **not** uniquely guilty of aggression — Sword marched on Tianjing; Demon harvested the siege; lesser powers burned each other everywhere. |
| **Provisional** | Pride and refusal to submit were **not** inherently foolish and were **not** heaven’s arbitrary punishment. For much of his life, confidence and ambition likely **fed** his success. Downfall came when those same qualities met an opponent **above** the category he had learned to fight. |
| **Interpretation** | Neither idiot villain, infallible strategist, nor purely selfless martyr. |

### After Tianjing — what he likely weighed *(interpretation)*

**Canon:** At the gates he **tested** the Half-Step, was **injured**, **withdrew**, and **refused to kneel**.

**Interpretation (likely strategic reasoning — not certain private thought):**

- After that clash he could judge outright victory was **extraordinarily unlikely** — but **not** literally impossible.
- He may still have believed in **meaningful outcomes short of victory**: wound the Half-Step, survive a punishing exchange, demonstrate that Phoenix remained too formidable to dissolve casually — improving **negotiating position** under the emerging Tian order.
- **Ember Crown Plain** may have been a last attempt to show **institutional strength** worth political respect and **some autonomy**, not necessarily a bid to **kill** the Tian cultivator.
- Failure mixed **miscalculation of power disparity** with **unwillingness to surrender pride** without **testing** that disparity once more at full muster.

**Open:** Whether he framed the stand to himself as sacrifice, defiance, or calculated gamble.

### Seven Days of Weight — what they were and were not

| Layer | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Canon** | ~**seven days** between the Tianjing garden walk / Flame Emperor’s withdrawal and the Half-Step’s march to **Ember Crown Plain**. Tianjing goes quiet; the continent **feels** the new ceiling. |
| **Canon** | The Half-Step uses the interval to **acclimate** and **explore** unprecedented Half-Step capacity — heaven’s weight, qi pressure, what the gate allows. Chroniclers: *stillness more terrifying than rage*. |
| **Must not imply** | He was helpless, unstable, or **needed** seven days to become strong enough to defeat the Flame Emperor. He was already **overwhelmingly** powerful; the week is **familiarization**, not a power-up timer. |
| **Open** | **Do not** invent a definitive ultimatum, secret negotiation, or hidden event during these seven days. |

### Tian brothers — counsel vs judgment

| Layer | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Canon** | The **elder brother** (emergency patriarch, later reigning emperor) was **consulted** on wider political consequences of the Flame Emperor’s continued defiance. An unbowed paragon of Phoenix expansionism was a **conspicuous obstacle** to a **credible continental settlement**. |
| **Canon** | The elder could **advise** on charter politics, precedent, and stability; the decision to **confront and execute** the Flame Emperor at Ember Crown belonged to the **younger brother** (Half-Step / founding Immortal) — not an imperial order handed down, not the elder wielding the younger as a weapon. |
| **Provisional** | The execution **demonstrated** that the era of **unrestricted conquest** had ended. Phoenix was singled out for **aggressive expansionism**, **continued resistance after Tianjing**, and the **political weight of its leader** — **not** arbitrary personal hatred of the sect. |
| **Interpretation** | The Half-Step may have **respected** the Flame Emperor’s determination without believing it acceptable to leave him **in power**. |

### Ember Crown — execution beat *(canon recap)*

Phoenix masses for **Realm Reforge** (#8). The Half-Step **arrives** after the Seven Days. He does **not** honor-duel. **Dragon’s Edict** (龙敕 — **working name**) or indistinguishable art: **one gesture**; Flame Emperor **executed before both armies**. See merged fork above.

### Flame Emperor — survival contingency *(provisional)*

**Provisional historical direction:**

The Flame Emperor could recognize that confronting the Half-Step might **cost his life**. While still allowing some chance of victory or meaningful resistance, he **may** have prepared Phoenix Court to **survive without him**:

- Continuity of cultivation inheritance
- Succession leaning on his **younger brother**
- Trusted elders briefed to hold institutions
- Critical resources guarded against leadership collapse

**Do not** invent named artefacts, secret techniques, coded final orders, or confirmed last conversations.

**Open:** Whether he **intended** the final stand as sacrifice.
